Sonic Mania looks like a game that jumped straight out of an old television, but it does not feel dusty at all. It is bright, fast, funny, and packed with tiny surprises. One moment Sonic is sprinting through a loop, then he is bouncing off springs, dodging spikes, chasing a giant ring, and somehow getting squashed by a moving wall. It can go from cool to chaos in seconds.

I reckon that is what makes it such a brilliant retro game. You do not need to remember the old Sonic games to enjoy it. The simple idea is still awesome today. Run towards the right, grab rings, find a path through the stage, and try not to crash into one of Doctor Eggman's weird robots.

## Three heroes with different tricks

You can play as Sonic, Tails, or Knuckles, and they do not all tackle a level the same way. Sonic is the speedy choice. Tails can fly for a short time, which is very handy when a platform looks just a bit too far away. Knuckles can glide and climb walls, so he can reach places that the others might race straight past.

Changing character makes familiar stages feel different. With Sonic, I want to keep my speed and launch off every ramp. With Tails, I start looking above the normal path for hidden platforms. With Knuckles, every tall wall suddenly looks like a possible route. It is clever because the game encourages another go without needing to change all the rules.

The movement also feels satisfyingly bouncy. Sonic builds speed down slopes and blasts up the other side. If I mistime a jump, the whole smooth run can turn into a pinball disaster. That is frustrating for a second, but it is usually funny enough that I want another try immediately.

## Old zones with new surprises

Sonic Mania mixes classic style with fresh ideas. Some zones are based on places longtime fans will recognise, while others bring new scenery and tricks. Even the familiar places have changed layouts, hazards, and boss battles, so they are not just copies.

The backgrounds are full of colour and movement. There are palm trees, flashing machines, watery tunnels, spinning platforms, and loads of little details rushing past. The pixel art is chunky enough to feel properly retro, but the animation is so lively that Sonic never seems stiff. He taps his foot when he is waiting and turns into a blur when he gets moving.

Every zone has more than one route as well. The highest path often feels fast and safe if you can keep your momentum. Lower paths can have extra enemies, water, or tricky jumps. Falling does not always mean you have failed. Sometimes it means you have discovered a whole different bit of the stage.

That makes exploring tempting, even in a game about speed. I like looking for giant rings because they can lead to special stages where Sonic chases a UFO. These sections test steering and speed in a completely different way. Normal checkpoints can also send you into spinning blue sphere challenges. They are simple to understand, but my brain starts panicking when the course gets quicker.

## Rings make mistakes less scary

Rings are one of the best ideas in platform games. If Sonic gets hit while carrying rings, they explode everywhere, but he gets another chance to keep going. You can scramble around and collect a few before they vanish. That little rescue mission creates heaps of comedy, especially after you had a huge collection and suddenly have almost nothing.

If you get hit without any rings, you lose a life. Spikes, crushing traps, and bottomless gaps can also stop a run quickly. Sonic Mania is family friendly, but it is not always easy. Later stages expect quick reactions and some patience. The bosses can be especially tricky because you need to watch their pattern instead of charging in wildly.

I think the challenge feels fair most of the time. A bad run teaches you where a trap is hiding. On the next attempt you can jump earlier, choose another route, or slow down for once. Yes, telling Sonic to slow down sounds ridiculous, but it genuinely helps.

## Couch chaos for two

Sonic Mania also has local play for two people. Competition Mode lets players race against each other, which is an excellent way to discover who can get lost the fastest. There is also a cooperative option with a friend, so someone can join the adventure rather than only watching.

Two player Sonic can be a bit wild because the screen follows the action and one player may zoom away. That is part of the fun in a lounge room. One person is trying to make perfect jumps while the other is yelling because they have disappeared off screen. It is not calm teamwork, but it is memorable teamwork.

## Why it still feels speedy

Sonic Mania was released in 2017, yet its retro look helps it feel timeless. It does not rely on realistic graphics. It relies on strong colours, catchy music, smooth controls, smart level design, and a blue hedgehog moving at a completely unreasonable speed.

It is also a neat way for younger players to understand why classic Sonic became so popular. You get the speedy loops and familiar characters, but the game has enough modern polish and new ideas to avoid feeling like homework about gaming history.

My favourite thing is the balance between racing and exploring. You can charge ahead like a legend, or poke around for giant rings and alternate paths. Both styles work, although rushing blindly usually ends with Sonic dropping every ring he owns.

Sonic Mania proves a game does not need a massive open world to feel exciting. Give me a colourful zone, a few ridiculous ramps, Tails flying somewhere useful, Knuckles climbing the wrong wall, and Doctor Eggman waiting with another strange machine. That is more than enough for one brilliant speedy adventure.
