Pikmin 4 is one of those games that looks super cute at first, then suddenly your brain is doing ten jobs at once and you feel like a tiny space captain with a very messy backyard. It came out for Nintendo Switch in July 2023, and it is about exploring a strange planet with little plant creatures called Pikmin. That sounds calm, but then a giant bug stomps out of the grass and half your squad starts panicking, so it becomes proper chaos in about three seconds.

I like games where you can muck around, but Pikmin 4 is cool because mucking around can still be clever. You are part of the Rescue Corps, and you land on a planet to find missing explorers. Instead of just running in and punching everything, you grow Pikmin, pluck them out of the ground, and throw them at jobs. Red Pikmin are good with fire, Yellow Pikmin can reach higher spots and handle electricity, Blue Pikmin can go in water, and there are more types that make puzzles feel different. It is like having a toolbox, except the tools have leaves on their heads and sometimes get eaten if you are not careful.

The best new mate is Oatchi, the rescue pup. He is not just there to look adorable, even though he definitely does. Oatchi can carry stuff, smash obstacles, swim later on, sniff out treasure, and rush into enemies. Riding on him with a squad of Pikmin feels really funny, like a tiny parade charging through a garden. I reckon Oatchi makes the game easier to understand too, because if everything goes wrong you can hop on and zoom away instead of watching your whole plan fall apart.

Treasure hunting is my favourite part. The planet is full of normal human things, but because your character is tiny, everything looks massive and mysterious. A toy, a fruit, or a little household item can become an important discovery. I love that feeling where you spot something shiny behind a ledge and instantly start thinking, right, how do I get that back to the ship? You might need the correct Pikmin type, enough helpers to carry it, or a safe path back without monsters snacking on everyone.

The game is also really good at making time feel exciting without being too stressful. During the day, you explore areas, collect treasure, find castaways, and try to get things done before night. It makes you plan ahead. If there are only a few minutes left, do you start a big fight, carry one more treasure, or gather everyone and play it safe? I have absolutely made the wrong choice in games like this before. That is part of the fun, because the next day you come back thinking you are a genius with a proper plan.

Caves are where Pikmin 4 gets extra interesting. You go underground into little puzzle areas with enemies, treasures, and surprises. Caves feel like mini adventures inside the main adventure. Sometimes they are calm and you just organise your squad. Other times, something huge drops in and you suddenly wish you had been more careful with your Pikmin. I like how caves make the world feel bigger, because the garden is not just grass and flowers. There are hidden places underneath it all.

There are also Dandori battles and challenges. Dandori basically means being organised and efficient, which sounds like something a teacher would say, but in Pikmin it means racing to collect things and make smart choices quickly. It is actually fun because you can see where you wasted time. Maybe you sent too many Pikmin to one job, forgot a shortcut, or left Oatchi standing around doing nothing. When you improve, it feels satisfying because you are not just stronger. You are smarter.

Night expeditions are another cool part. They change the feeling of the game because everything is darker and more dangerous. Instead of normal exploring, you are defending glowing mounds using Glow Pikmin. It feels a bit spooky but not too scary, which is good. It is more like backyard monsters are having a wild party and you have to stop them from wrecking your stuff.

What I reckon makes Pikmin 4 kid friendly is that it is colourful and silly, but it still respects your brain. You have to think about routes, squads, timing, enemy weaknesses, and what order to do things in. It is strategy, but not the boring kind where you stare at menus forever. You are always moving, throwing Pikmin, whistling them back, riding Oatchi, and trying not to make a huge mistake.

It can be a bit sad when Pikmin get left behind or eaten, because they are tiny and loyal and just trying their best. But that also makes you care more. You start whistling everyone back before doing dangerous stuff, and you feel proud when you finish a day with heaps of treasure and your squad safe.

Pikmin 4 is awesome because it turns a garden into a giant planet full of treasure, monsters, puzzles, and tiny teamwork. It is cute, clever, and chaotic in a way that makes you want to do one more day. If you like games where planning actually matters, but you still want silly creatures and a space dog, this one is a ripper.
