A suspicious sign points towards the river. Behind it sits a barrel containing one feather and a clue about a very noisy bird. Your friends race off looking for the next hiding place, while you try not to laugh at the chicken following them.

That is all a Minecraft treasure hunt needs to get started: a world, a trail of clues, and something fun at the end.

## What you need

You need access to a Minecraft Realm, a shared online world that invited players can enter even when its owner is away. Ask a parent or guardian before starting or changing a Realm subscription, inviting players, or sharing anything online.

Only invite people you know. Java Edition players need a Java Realm, while Bedrock Edition players need a Bedrock Realm. Bedrock supports play across compatible consoles, mobile devices, and Windows computers. Java supports Windows, Linux, and macOS, but Java and Bedrock players cannot join the same Realm.

Bring signs, books and quills, barrels or chests, and a prize. A renamed cookie, a colourful banner, or a silly trophy is better than rare gear that could start an argument.

## Make the hunt

1. Choose a safe starting spot near everyone's base. Put down a sign explaining the goal and the rules. Tell players not to break blocks unless a clue says they can.

2. Pick four to six landmarks. Good choices include a bridge, farm, library, statue, or strange tree. Keep every clue in an easy to reach area. Avoid lava, deep caves, and long trips through the Nether.

3. Write one clue for each landmark. Make it puzzling but fair. "Find the room where paper becomes knowledge" could lead to a library. Put the next clue in a clearly visible barrel there.

4. Test the complete route yourself. Check that every clue points to the right place, every container opens, and no mob can wreck the trail. Use torches around the route if players will hunt at night.

5. Hide the final prize and add a sign naming the winners. If several friends are playing, include enough small prizes for everyone who reaches the end.

6. Invite your friends, agree on a start time, and release the hunters together. The Realm owner does not need to stay online, but joining in means you can help if a clue causes total confusion.

## Add one clever twist

Give each player a different coloured wool block at the start. Halfway through the hunt, make a clue that only works when everyone places their wool on matching spots. Suddenly the race becomes teamwork.

Keep a backup of the world before the event, especially if your clues use valuable builds. Then enjoy the chase. The best moment is usually not finding the prize. It is watching somebody confidently sprint in the completely wrong direction because they decided a chicken was definitely part of the puzzle.
