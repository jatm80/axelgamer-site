An enchanting table surrounded by bookshelves looks like a tiny magical library. The floating symbols are cool, but the useful bit is hidden in the menu: 15 connected bookshelves unlock enchantment choices with a level requirement as high as 30.

The setup works in both Java Edition and Bedrock Edition. Enchantments are random, so a level 30 choice can still surprise you.

## What you need

* One enchanting table
* 15 bookshelves
* Lapis lazuli
* An item that can be enchanted
* At least 30 experience levels for the highest choice

Experience levels are the green number above your hotbar. Lapis lazuli is the blue mineral found while mining.

## Build the bookshelf setup

1. Place the enchanting table where you want the middle of your room.

2. Leave one full block of air around the table. That empty gap is where you stand and where the table connects to nearby shelves.

3. Place bookshelves on the other side of the gap. Shelves can sit at the same height as the table or one block higher. Do not put them in the corners only, because diagonal shelves do not connect.

4. Keep a doorway so you can reach the table. Build a low row around three sides, then add more shelves on top until you have 15 connected shelves.

5. Keep the gap clear. A torch or another solid block between the table and a shelf can block its power. Put lighting on the wall or ceiling instead.

## Test the table

1. Use the table and place your item in the first slot.

2. Put at least three lapis lazuli in the blue slot. Three choices should appear.

3. Check the bottom choice. With all 15 shelves connected, it can ask for level 30. If the number is lower, recount the shelves and check the air gap for stray blocks.

You need 30 levels to select a level 30 choice, but it does not remove all 30. The bottom option costs three levels and three lapis. The level number is a requirement, while the smaller lapis number is the amount you pay.

## Avoid wasting a favourite tool

Hover over a choice to preview one possible enchantment. The item may receive extra enchantments too, but the preview does not promise them. If none of the choices looks useful, enchant a cheap book or another item to refresh the list.

Start with a pickaxe, sword or armour piece you are happy to use. The result is random, and even the fanciest library cannot guarantee the exact enchantment you wanted. Opening the menu is exciting because you never quite know what will appear.
