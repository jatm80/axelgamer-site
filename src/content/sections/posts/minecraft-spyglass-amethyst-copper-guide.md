A tiny purple crystal can turn two bits of copper into one of Minecraft's handiest exploring tools. A spyglass lets you zoom in on distant places, which is brilliant when you are deciding whether that dark shape is a village, a tree or one extremely suspicious pillager.

The recipe and basic controls work in both Java Edition and Bedrock Edition.

## What you need

* One amethyst shard
* Two copper ingots
* One crafting table

Copper ore is common underground. Mine it with a stone pickaxe or better, then smelt the raw copper in a furnace to make ingots.

Amethyst shards grow inside amethyst geodes. These round underground structures have an outer layer of smooth basalt and a middle layer of calcite. Inside, look for amethyst clusters growing from budding amethyst blocks. Break a fully grown cluster with an iron pickaxe or better to collect four shards. Leave the budding blocks where they are because they cannot be collected normally and will grow more crystals later.

## Craft your spyglass

1. Open a crafting table.

2. Put the amethyst shard in the top middle square.

3. Place one copper ingot directly below it, then put the second copper ingot in the bottom middle square.

4. Move the spyglass into your inventory.

5. Hold it and use it. Your view will zoom through a round frame. Stop using it to return to normal.

## Try three useful scouting tricks

Climb a hill near your base and scan the horizon before travelling. You might spot a village roof, ruined portal or unusual biome without using food on a long walk in the wrong direction.

Take a spyglass on ocean trips too. Zooming from a boat can help you inspect coastlines and islands before steering closer. Do not stare through it while drifting into rocks or underwater ruins.

In a large cave, stand somewhere secure and check ledges across the gap. You may notice ore, an exit or a mob before making a risky bridge. Put down torches and watch your own platform as well. Zooming makes faraway objects easier to see, but it hides much of the area around you.

A spyglass does not reveal blocks through walls or make the world load farther than your normal view distance. It simply magnifies what is already visible. Keep one beside your map and food, then use it whenever the horizon looks interesting.
