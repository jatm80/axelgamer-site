You flick a tiny lever at one end of your base. A lamp glows at the other end. It looks like a simple trick, but this build teaches one of the most useful redstone rules in Minecraft.

Redstone is Minecraft's version of electrical wiring. It does not use real electricity, so you can safely experiment without turning your blocky bedroom into toast.

This build works in Java Edition and Bedrock Edition. The basic parts and signal limit are the same in both.

## What you need

For a short practice build, grab one lever, one redstone lamp, about 20 redstone dust, one redstone repeater, and some solid blocks. Creative mode is easiest while learning.

In Survival mode, craft a lever with a stick and cobblestone. A redstone lamp needs one glowstone block surrounded by four redstone dust. A repeater needs three stone, two redstone torches, and one redstone dust.

## Build the light switch

1. Make a straight row of 21 solid blocks on the floor. Leave room to walk beside it so you can see what happens.

2. Attach the lever to the side of the first block.

3. Put redstone dust on top of blocks 1 to 15. Placed dust becomes redstone wire.

4. Stand near the lever and face towards the far end. Place the repeater on block 16. The little arrow on top must point towards the lamp. A repeater only sends a signal one way.

5. Put more redstone dust on blocks 17 to 20.

6. Place the redstone lamp on block 21 so the last piece of dust touches it.

7. Flick the lever. The redstone line should glow and the lamp should switch on. Flick it again to turn everything off.

## Why the repeater matters

A redstone signal can travel through only 15 blocks of dust before it runs out. You can watch the glowing wire become darker as it gets farther from the lever.

The repeater boosts that fading signal back to full strength. Without it, the lamp at the end of this build stays dark. For a much longer hallway, add another correctly facing repeater before each signal runs out.

If the lamp does not glow, check the repeater first. Break it and place it again while facing the lamp. Also look for gaps in the dust or blocks that interrupt the path.

Use the repeater to change the timing too. Press or tap it to move its small torch and add a little more delay. Chain several repeaters together and your lamp can blink on noticeably later. That is the first step towards timed doors, warning lights, and plenty of clever redstone nonsense.
