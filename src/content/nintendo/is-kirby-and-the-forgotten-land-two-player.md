**Yes. Kirby and the Forgotten Land supports two-player local co-op on one Nintendo Switch. Player one is Kirby and player two is Bandana Waddle Dee.** Nintendo describes sharing Joy-Con controllers for this mode on its [original Switch game page](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/kirby-and-the-forgotten-land-switch/).

## Player two is a partner, not another Kirby

Bandana Waddle Dee has a different role from Kirby. That matters if two children both expect to use Kirby's transformations: co-op gives them two characters, rather than two identical sets of abilities. Agree to swap roles between stages if both players want a turn as Kirby.

For a parent and child, let the child choose which character interests them. Helping does not have to mean taking the leading role. Ask whether they want advice, help during a fight, or simply someone exploring beside them.

## Make the first session manageable

Nintendo offers Spring-Breeze mode with more health and easier enemies, alongside the tougher Wild mode. Start with Spring-Breeze if either player is learning to move around a 3D space. A sensible first goal is finishing a stage together; hunting every hidden objective can wait.

The free original-game demo includes three stages and lets you try local co-op. That is a useful way to check whether the shared screen and different character roles suit your family before buying. Local co-op is the feature to look for here; an online-feature label is not a promise of internet story co-op.

## Check the edition before buying

This guide concerns the original Nintendo Switch game. [Nintendo's Switch 2 Edition + Star-Crossed World](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/kirby-and-the-forgotten-land-nintendo-switch-2-edition-plus-star-crossed-world-switch-2/) adds a new story and visual improvements and requires Nintendo Switch 2. Do not buy that edition for an original Switch, Switch OLED, or Switch Lite.
